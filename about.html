About

Sam Chambers, Ph.D.

Environmental geographer and biometeorologist working at the intersection of climate and health, border geographies, landscape ecology, and spatial science.

Biography

Research across movement, exposure, and landscape.

My research examines how environmental conditions, infrastructure, and governance shape human and ecological exposure, mobility, and survival.

I combine spatial analysis, environmental modeling, remote sensing, and process-based approaches across several connected domains. In biometeorology and climate-health research, I study how heat, humidity, radiation, terrain, activity, and access to resources shape physiological risk. In border studies, I use geospatial and counter-forensic methods to investigate migration, surveillance, mortality, disappearance, and environmental exposure. In landscape ecology, I examine how terrain, land-use change, energy development, and infrastructure influence wildlife movement, energetic costs, and habitat connectivity.

Across these areas, I am interested in difficult-to-observe geographic processes: hazards that vary through time, movement that leaves incomplete traces, infrastructure whose effects extend beyond its physical footprint, and environmental conditions whose consequences emerge through interaction rather than any single variable.
