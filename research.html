Research

Spatial questions.
Environmental mechanisms.

I develop and apply geographic methods to understand how heterogeneous environments and infrastructure alter exposure, movement, accessibility, and survival.

01
Biometeorology & Climate

How does weather become physiological strain when people move through real landscapes?

My biometeorology research links meteorological conditions, radiation, humidity, wind, terrain, land cover, activity, and exposure duration to human thermal response. I use spatially explicit heat-balance modeling to move beyond air temperature alone and examine how environmental conditions become consequential for people working, traveling, or living in hazardous heat.

Current work includes extreme-heat survivability, exposure typologies, urban climate and health, cooling and accessibility, and the role of physical exertion in heat-related illness and mortality.

02
Borders, Mobility & Environment

How do enforcement systems reorganize movement and environmental exposure across border landscapes?

My border research combines geospatial analysis, environmental modeling, archival and spatial evidence, and counter-forensic approaches to study migration, surveillance, mortality, disappearance, and exposure. Much of this work focuses on the U.S.–Mexico border, with collaborative investigations extending to other militarized border regions.

I am particularly interested in how infrastructure and policy alter the geography of mobility: not only where people move, but the terrain, thermal conditions, isolation, and physiological costs encountered along the way.

03
Landscape & Spatial Analytics

How do terrain, development, and fragmentation change the energetic and spatial costs of movement?

My landscape research uses GPS tracking, remote sensing, terrain data, movement models, and energetic approaches to examine wildlife mobility and habitat connectivity. This work includes jaguar, mule deer, pronghorn, and Texas tortoise, with emphasis on how development and infrastructure reshape functional landscapes.

Across human and ecological applications, I use GIS, remote sensing, spatiotemporal analysis, machine learning, and process-based models to reconstruct patterns that are only partly observable.
